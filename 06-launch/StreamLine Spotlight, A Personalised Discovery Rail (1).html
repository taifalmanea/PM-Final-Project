StreamLine Spotlight

A Personalised Discovery Rail

Turn an overwhelming home screen into 30-minute sessions with a Spotlight rail that tells each Explorer why they will love a title.

05Strategy
Problem · evidence · value · hypothesis

"Nothing to play" ends the session before it starts.

StreamLine is a streaming app with a catalog of shows, music and podcasts.

Problem

Casual Explorers open StreamLine to unwind, but the home screen gives them rows of titles with no reason to choose any of them. In our exit survey (n = 1,200), "nothing to play" was the #1 reason for short sessions, ahead of "app too slow" and "already played everything."

Casual Explorer: opens StreamLine 3+ times a week, mostly in the evening, with no title in mind, and starts fewer than 20% of sessions from search.

Value proposition

Spotlight replaces endless scrolling with six titles picked for you, each with a one-line reason why you'll love it, so choosing takes seconds instead of minutes.

Why it matters

Short-session Explorers retain at half the 30-day rate of 30-minute-session Explorers ◇, which suggests the first session matters. The test's retention guardrail will show whether the link is causal.

11→13%Target · 14 days
Hypothesis (assumption-based)

Because Explorers hesitate when titles come without a reason, we believe a Spotlight rail with a one-line reason per title will raise the share of Explorer sessions that start from discovery and last 30+ minutes from 11% to 13% within 14 days, without reducing 7-day retention by more than 2pt.

08Validation
Experiment plan

Three arms, so we learn whether the reason is the product.

A · Control · 33%

Current home screen

B · Rail only · 33%

Spotlight rail, no reasons

C · Rail + reason · 33%

Spotlight rail with one-line reasons

What each comparison tells us

C vs A · Does Spotlight work at all? This is the ship decision.

C vs B · Do the reasons add value beyond a prominent shortlist? This tests the core insight.

Primary metric: of all Casual Explorer sessions, the % that start from a home-screen discovery surface and last 30+ minutes. Randomised by user.

Guardrail & checks

7-day retention must not drop by 2pt or more in any arm vs. control · Secondary: share of sub-10-minute sessions, time-to-first-play · Sample-ratio check on day 2 · Novelty check: compare week-1 vs. week-2 lift.

Ship

C beats A by ≥ 2pt (significant), retention drop < 2pt, and week-2 lift is at least half of week-1.

Iterate

Lift is positive but under 2pt or not significant, or C ≈ B (reasons add nothing): rework the reason copy and retest.

Kill

Retention drops by 2pt or more, or the primary metric falls vs. control.

10Story
Friction · aha moment · next

The reason copy, not the algorithm, was the product.

Writing the one-line "why you will love this" reason forced real clarity on the persona, the aha moment was realising the reason copy, not the algorithm, was the product. The hardest part was resisting the urge to redesign the whole home screen.

That insight reshaped the project: the PRD now specifies the reason with a quality bar, and the experiment has a rail-only arm to test it.

My belief going into the test

A tight, reasoned shortlist beats a longer list. The C vs. B comparison will show whether I'm right.

What I would do next
  • Close the loop, feed Spotlight engagement back into the taste model.
  • Test reason-copy variants: specific vs. mood-based reasons.
  • A/B test social-proof badges.

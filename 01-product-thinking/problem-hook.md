# Problem Hook & Value Proposition (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own):** StreamLine Spotlight
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** If StreamLine does nothing for 12 months, it risks becoming increasingly irrelevant to quality-focused viewers as competitors win those users through stronger curation and more distinctive content. Its large catalog becomes less of a competitive advantage and more of a source of choice overload.
- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** Users are likely leaving StreamLine to Google reviews, browse competitor platforms, or rely on social recommendations to figure out what is actually worth watching instead of discovering something confidently within StreamLine.
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** StreamLine has plenty to watch, but users can’t easily find what’s worth watching putting engagement and retention at risk.
- **Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** Spotlight turns StreamLine from a warehouse of 15,000 titles into a curated destination for quality content, helping users quickly discover what is worth watching and giving StreamLine a stronger reason to stay competitive now.

## Cold-read your own hook
- **Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:** Yes, but it could be sharper. “Engagement plateauing” and losing quality-focused users are meaningful business risks, but the current hook sounds somewhat gradual rather than urgent. The stronger risk is user churn and weakening competitive position if StreamLine remains difficult to navigate despite its large catalog.
- **Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?:** Systemic. With 15,000 titles, difficulty finding worthwhile content is likely a recurring discovery problem for a meaningful segment of users not an isolated failure. The fact that users leave the product to search elsewhere reinforces that the problem sits in the core viewing journey.
- **Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?:** Yes. The obstacle is not a lack of content; it is knowing what is worth watching. Spotlight directly addresses that by turning the large catalog into curated recommendations. It is more than “add recommendations” because the strategic shift is from volume → curated quality.
